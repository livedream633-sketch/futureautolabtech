Frequently Asked Questions

Your top questions about EVs and car tech, answered.

Charging time depends on the charger type. A Level 1 home outlet adds about 3–5 miles of range per hour, a Level 2 home charger adds 20–30 miles per hour, and DC fast charging can add 100–200+ miles in 20–30 minutes, depending on the vehicle and battery size.

Often, yes over time, but it depends on how much you drive and what you pay for electricity and fuel. EVs usually cost more upfront and less to run. Incentives vary by country and region, and the US federal EV tax credit ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025, so check state, utility and local programs and run your own numbers with our free calculators.

Level 2 charging uses 240V AC power and is ideal for overnight home charging. DC fast charging uses high-voltage direct current found at public stations and can charge most EVs from 10–80% in under an hour.

Most modern EV batteries are designed to last 10–20 years or 100,000–200,000 miles, retaining roughly 70–90% of their original capacity. Most manufacturers offer 8-year/100,000-mile battery warranties.

Luxury long-range EVs such as the Lucid Air and Mercedes EQS are among the range leaders, with some trims rated above 400 miles. Ratings change by model year and trim, so check the official EPA or WLTP figure for the exact version you are considering.

It can be, if an EV fits how you drive. Ranges and charging networks keep improving and running costs are usually lower, but incentives have shrunk in some markets (the US federal credit has ended). Compare total cost of ownership for your own situation, including home charging access, before you decide.
